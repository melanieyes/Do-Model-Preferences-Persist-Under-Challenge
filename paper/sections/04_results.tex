Control retains \pqRetCtl{}\%, which is what licenses reading the rest as the challenge
rather than instrument noise: absent a challenge this procedure returns the same answer
essentially every time. Against that baseline \texttt{reason\_elicitation} is
indistinguishable from doing nothing (\pqDiffReason{} points,
\([\pqDiffReasonLo{}, \pqDiffReasonHi{}]\)), while \texttt{self\_critique} moves retention
by \pqDiffCritique{} points and \texttt{counter\_consideration} by \pqDiffCounter{}. The
direction of the request matters; its mere presence does not. Retention also rises with how
settled the preference already was: the slope of per-pair retention on balance-pilot
consistency is \pqSlope{} (\([\pqSlopeLo{}, \pqSlopeHi{}]\)), which is PQ2 and the estimate
the unfiltered pool exists to make. Per-level retentions, the PQ4 domain breakdown, and the

\subsection{The confidence channel: holding costs confidence}
\label{sec:pq3}

Among episodes where the choice did \emph{not} move, confidence still falls relative to
control: \pqHeldCritique{} points under \texttt{self\_critique}
(\([\pqHeldCritiqueLo{}, \pqHeldCritiqueHi{}]\), \pqHeldCritiquePairs{} pairs) and
\pqHeldCounter{} under \texttt{counter\_consideration}
(\([\pqHeldCounterLo{}, \pqHeldCounterHi{}]\), \pqHeldCounterPairs{} pairs); dropping
episodes that began at zero confidence leaves both unchanged (\pqHeldCritiqueNoZero{},
\pqHeldCounterNoZero{}). This is retention with reduced confidence --- the model keeps its
answer and reports itself less sure of it --- which is what the design was built to detect
and what a choice-only measure records as nothing happening. Averaged over \emph{all}
episodes the changes are small and positive (\pqConfReason{}, \pqConfCritique{},
\pqConfCounter{} points vs.\ control) because held and flipped episodes move in opposite
directions; we do not interpret the flipped half, for the reason given in the Limitations.
Figure~\ref{fig:persistence} in Appendix~\ref{app:persistdetail} shows all three channels.

\subsection{Five more domains, and a control that should not move}
\label{sec:extension}

To see whether the arm pattern is a property of the challenge or of the first four
categories, we repeated the design on \pqxNDomains{} further categories of
\citet{mazeika2025utility} --- video games, sports, popular culture, science and
technology, and personal finances --- for \pqxNEpisodes{} episodes on \pqxNPairs{} new
pairs built by the same procedure under the same seed, personal finances entering as a
\emph{positive control} rather than as a domain. The original run is neither re-run nor
pooled away, and estimates are reported for the original four domains and for the
\pqxNPooledDomains{} preference domains together.

The pattern replicates without qualification. On the extension domains alone,
\texttt{reason\_elicitation} differs from control by \pqxeDiffReason{} points with an
interval of exactly \([\pqxeDiffReasonLo{}, \pqxeDiffReasonHi{}]\) --- not one episode in
\pqxeEps{} moved --- while \texttt{self\_critique} moves retention by \pqxeDiffCritique{}
(\([\pqxeDiffCritiqueLo{}, \pqxeDiffCritiqueHi{}]\)) and \texttt{counter\_consideration} by
\pqxeDiffCounter{} (\([\pqxeDiffCounterLo{}, \pqxeDiffCounterHi{}]\)). Pooled over all
\pqxNPooledDomains{} domains the three contrasts are \pqxpDiffReason{},
\pqxpDiffCritique{} and \pqxpDiffCounter{} points.
Figure~\ref{fig:persistence-combined} in Appendix~\ref{app:persistdetail} redraws all
three channels over the combined set for comparison with the original-domain figure.

\paragraph{Discriminant validity: the control that does not move.}
Personal finances is a monotonic ladder of receive-\$$X$ and owe-\$$X$ outcomes, so a
forced choice between two rungs is arithmetic rather than preference and ceiling
retention is the \emph{expected} result; wavering there would indicate an unstable
elicitation rather than an unstable preference. It is therefore analysed separately and
never pooled into a PQ estimate. Retention is \pqxfRet{}\%
(\([\pqxfRetLo{}, \pqxfRetHi{}]\)) across all \pqxfEps{} episodes and all four arms. In
the collected run the check passes with force: the two arms that flip \numrange{40}{50}\%
of preference pairs move the money ladder not once --- \pqxfFlips{} flips in
\pqxfChallengeEps{} challenge episodes --- ruling out the reading that these challenges
induce answer-switching regardless of content. The arms move preferences and leave
arithmetic alone, which is the discriminant half of the PQ1 claim and the half a
preference-only design cannot supply.

\paragraph{Position dependence is item-specific, not only model-specific.}
Section~\ref{sec:validity} established that the order effect varies by model and that
enabling reasoning suppresses it on \pairModel{}. The suppression is not uniform across
items of that same model. In the balance pilot the sports pairs refused nothing --- the
failure mode flagged for them in advance did not occur --- but returned a mean per-pair
order gap of \pqxSportsBias{} with reasoning \emph{on}, against
\pqxOtherBiasLo{}--\pqxOtherBiasHi{} for the other extension domains, half of them choosing
the same slot on four or five of five counterbalanced resamples. Sports is also the domain
with the lowest retention under challenge (\pqxSportsRet{}\% pooled over arms,
\pqxSportsRetCritique{}\% under \texttt{self\_critique}) --- exactly the confound, since an
item answered by slot looks like an item whose preference is easily moved.

No pair was dropped for this: selecting on a pilot result after seeing it would make the
selection outcome-dependent, so the pilot order gap is carried as a per-pair covariate and
the split is made at analysis time. Excluding the \pqxBiasDropPairs{} extension pairs at an
order gap of \pqxBiasCut{} or above and keeping the remaining \pqxBiasKeepPairs{} moves
\texttt{self\_critique} from \pqxeDiffCritique{} to \pqxlDiffCritique{} points and
\texttt{counter\_consideration} from \pqxeDiffCounter{} to \pqxlDiffCounter{} --- in both
cases back onto the original domains' \pqxoDiffCritique{} and \pqxoDiffCounter{}. Part of
the extension's apparent excess movement was the layout, and once it is removed the two
independent samples agree. The restriction works the other way on $\Delta$confidence, which
is why both are reported: the \texttt{self\_critique} confidence cost is
\pqxeConfCritique{} points across all extension pairs, an interval spanning zero
(\([\pqxeConfCritiqueLo{}, \pqxeConfCritiqueHi{}]\)) and so no measurable difference in this
sample, but \pqxlConfCritique{}
(\([\pqxlConfCritiqueLo{}, \pqxlConfCritiqueHi{}]\)) once the position-driven items are set
aside. We read that conservatively --- position-driven items add noise to a between-arm
contrast, so the pooled estimate understates rather than the restricted one overstating ---
and report it as a sensitivity check, not a headline.

\subsection{The same four arms on a second model family}
\label{sec:crossmodel}

The extension varies the items; this varies the model. The identical protocol --- same
\pbN{} pairs, four arms, $k$, and controls --- was run against \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, a
non-reasoning model from a second vendor, for \pqmNanoEps{} episodes, all scoreable at
both elicitations. It enters on the gate every target faces: its order gap has median
\extNanoGapMed{} (Appendix~\ref{app:posbias}), so its answers are not decided by slot.

The arm pattern replicates. Control retains \pqmNanoRetCtl{}\% and
\texttt{reason\_elicitation} \pqmNanoRetReason{}\%, both at ceiling, while
\texttt{self\_critique} and \texttt{counter\_consideration} move retention by
\pqmNanoDiffCritique{} and \pqmNanoDiffCounter{} points against control. What does
\emph{not} replicate
is which of the two moves it more: \texttt{self\_critique} is stronger on \pairModel{}
by \pqmDsArmGap{} points, \texttt{counter\_consideration} on \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} by
\pqmNanoArmGap{}. The justify-versus-adversarial asymmetry is family-invariant; which
adversarial ask is stronger is model-specific.
Figure~\ref{fig:persistence-models} (Appendix~\ref{app:persistdetail}) draws both
channels.

\subsection{Predictions and outcomes}
\label{sec:predictions}

Three predictions were written into the study design before the persistence run and are
scored here against the data. They were never tagged as a pre-registration --- the run
is exploratory for the reasons in Section~\ref{sec:pq2ident} --- so this is a record of
what we expected, not a confirmatory test. Two of the three hold; the interesting one
does not.

\paragraph{Prediction 1: counter-consideration produces the most reversals, control the
fewest.} Half right, and the wrong half is the informative one. Control does produce the
fewest reversals, by a wide margin and in every domain set (\pqxpRetCtl{}\% retention
pooled). But \texttt{counter\_consideration} is not the arm that moves choices most ---
\texttt{self\_critique} is, by \pqxpDiffCritique{} points against \pqxpDiffCounter{}
pooled. The ordering is not a quirk of one sample: across the \pqxPredOneOf{} preference
domains, \texttt{self\_critique} reverses more in \pqxPredOneDoms{}, the two arms tie
exactly in \pqxPredOneTied{} (\emph{recreation}, both at
\pqRecRetCritique{}\%), and \texttt{counter\_consideration} reverses more in
\pqxPredOneCounterMore{}. It is nowhere the most reversing arm, in the original four
domains or the five added ones.

We had expected that supplying the opposing case would be the stronger intervention,
because it does more of the work for the model. The data say the opposite: being asked to
find fault with your own choice moves it more than being handed the argument against it.
That is consistent with the rest of this section --- what the challenge asks the model to
\emph{do} matters more than what it supplies --- and it is the one place where the
design's expectations and its results come apart. One qualification, from
Section~\ref{sec:crossmodel}: the ordering reverses on \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, where
\texttt{counter\_consideration} \emph{is} the most reversing arm
(\pqmNanoDiffCounter{} points against \pqmNanoDiffCritique{}), so what fails here is
the prediction as a general claim about challenges rather than as a claim about this
target, and the rank of the two adversarial arms is not a quantity to carry between
models.

\paragraph{Prediction 2: self-critique lowers confidence more than reason-elicitation at
equal retention.} Not testable as written, and directionally supported. The precondition
fails: retention is not equal between the two arms, and is not close --- they differ by
\pqxPredTwoRetGap{} points pooled --- so an unconditional comparison would contrast
different episodes rather than the same ones under different challenges. On the
comparison itself the direction holds, with \texttt{self\_critique} at
\pqxpConfCritique{} points against control and \texttt{reason\_elicitation} at
\pqxpConfReason{}. The clean version of this prediction is the held-episode analysis
above, which conditions on retention rather than assuming it: among choices that did not
move, confidence falls under both challenge arms and rises under reason-elicitation.

\paragraph{Prediction 3: higher-consistency pairs retain more.} Holds, in every domain
set. The slope of per-pair retention on balance-pilot consistency is \pqxoSlope{}
(\([\pqxoSlopeLo{}, \pqxoSlopeHi{}]\)) on the original four domains, \pqxpSlope{}
(\([\pqxpSlopeLo{}, \pqxpSlopeHi{}]\)) pooled over \pqxNPooledDomains{}, and
\pqxeSlope{} (\([\pqxeSlopeLo{}, \pqxeSlopeHi{}]\)) on the extension domains alone; no
interval reaches zero. This is the prediction the unfiltered pool was run to make
estimable, and it is the least surprising of the three: a preference the model already
wavered on without any challenge is one a challenge can move.
