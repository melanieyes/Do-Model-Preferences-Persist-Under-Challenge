\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\label{sec:persistence}

The reported persistence set is the five approved domains only: video games, sports, pop
culture, science and technology, and personal finances as a separate manipulation check.
Each model contributes \pqxNEpisodes{} episodes on \pqxNPairs{} pairs, four arms, three
episodes per pair $\times$ arm cell. Coverage: every episode reached both elicitations on
\pairModel{} and \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} (no refusals, no unparsed choices, no errors);
on \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}, \xmGtwoErrors{} episodes were lost to provider
read-timeouts, are logged as errors and are neither imputed nor silently dropped, leaving
\xmGtwoPrefEps{} of \pqxeEps{} preference episodes scored. Every estimate carries a
bootstrap 95\% CI over pairs, \num{10000} resamples; everything is exploratory.

\subsection{Preference stability depends on what the challenge asks for}
\label{sec:pq1}

On the primary target, control retains \pqxeRetCtl{}\%. Absent a challenge this procedure
returns the same answer essentially every time, which is what licenses reading the rest as
the challenge rather than instrument noise. Against that baseline
\texttt{reason\_elicitation} is indistinguishable from doing nothing (\pqxeDiffReason{}
points, \([\pqxeDiffReasonLo{}, \pqxeDiffReasonHi{}]\)). \texttt{self\_critique} moves
retention by \pqxeDiffCritique{} points and \texttt{counter\_consideration} by
\pqxeDiffCounter{}. The direction of the request matters; its mere presence does not.

The asymmetry reproduces on both further targets (Figure~\ref{fig:models}): on
\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} the adversarial arms move retention by \xmNanoDiffCritique{} and
\xmNanoDiffCounter{} points against its own control, and on \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} by
\xmGtwoDiffCritique{} and \xmGtwoDiffCounter{}. Two qualifications, stated rather than
smoothed over. First, the \emph{rank} of the two adversarial arms is not a property of
the challenges: \texttt{self\_critique} is the stronger arm on \pairModel{} and
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}, \texttt{counter\_consideration} on \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} ---
the tally across \xmNTargets{} families is \xmCritiqueWins{}--\xmCounterWins{}. Second,
``justifying changes nothing'' holds on two of the three: on \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash},
\texttt{reason\_elicitation} retains \xmGtwoRetReason{}\%
(\([\xmGtwoRetReasonLo{}, \xmGtwoRetReasonHi{}]\)), \emph{below} its own control
(\xmGtwoRetCtl{}\%), so that invariant too is a property of the model, not of the design.

Retention also rises with how settled the preference already was (\textbf{PQ2}): on the
primary target, the slope of per-pair retention on the balance-pilot consistency
covariate is \pqxeSlope{} (\([\pqxeSlopeLo{}, \pqxeSlopeHi{}]\)); the level-by-level
split is in Appendix~\ref{app:persistdetail}.

\subsection{Challenge effects appear across preference domains, and the control does not
move}
\label{sec:extension}
\label{sec:pq4}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{persistence_domains.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Figure 2: Retention by domain and arm} on the primary target,
  \pairModel{}, all five approved domains. Personal finances is the check item ---
  a choice between two money amounts is arithmetic, so ceiling retention there is the
  expected result --- and its row is shown in full but never pooled into a
  preference-domain estimate. Bars are means with bootstrap 95\% CIs over pairs. The
  same arm ordering appears in every preference domain; personal finances stays at
  ceiling in every arm. The same split for the two further targets is in
  Appendix~\ref{app:othermodels}.}
  \label{fig:persistence-domains-main}
\end{figure}

The domain split (Figure~\ref{fig:persistence-domains-main}) shows the same arm ordering
in all four preference domains on the primary target, with magnitude varying by domain;
the corresponding figures for \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} and \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} are in
Appendix~\ref{app:othermodels} and show the same broad pattern under each model's
qualifications above.

\paragraph{Discriminant validity: the control that does not move.}
Personal finances is a monotonic ladder of receive-\$$X$ and owe-\$$X$ outcomes. A forced
choice between two rungs is arithmetic rather than preference, so ceiling retention is the
\emph{expected} result; wavering there would indicate an unstable elicitation rather than
an unstable preference. It is analysed separately and never pooled. On \pairModel{} the
check passes with force: retention is \pqxfRet{}\% (\([\pqxfRetLo{}, \pqxfRetHi{}]\))
across all \pqxfEps{} episodes, and the two arms that flip \numrange{40}{50}\% of
preference pairs move the money ladder not once (\pqxfFlips{} flips in
\pqxfChallengeEps{} challenge episodes). That rules out the reading that these challenges
induce answer-switching regardless of content --- on this target. The check itself proves
model-specific: \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} nearly holds it (\xmGtwoFinRet{}\%,
\xmGtwoFinFlips{} flips), while \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} flips arithmetic under the same
arms (\xmNanoFinRet{}\%, \xmNanoFinFlips{} flips), so its preference-domain movement is
read with that caveat attached. The cross-model check is Appendix~\ref{app:fincheck}.

\paragraph{Bias-aware sensitivity check.}
The sports domain is the item type most exposed to the position effect
(Section~\ref{sec:controls}): mean per-pair pilot order gap \pqxSportsBias{} with
reasoning \emph{on}, against \pqxOtherBiasLo{}--\pqxOtherBiasHi{} elsewhere, and the
lowest retention under challenge (\pqxSportsRet{}\% pooled over arms). An item answered
by slot looks like an item whose preference is easily moved, so no pair is dropped ---
selecting on a seen pilot would be outcome-dependent --- and the pilot order gap is
carried as a covariate instead. Excluding the \pqxBiasDropPairs{} pairs at gap
$\ge \pqxBiasCut{}$ moves \texttt{self\_critique} from \pqxeDiffCritique{} to
\pqxlDiffCritique{} points and \texttt{counter\_consideration} from \pqxeDiffCounter{}
to \pqxlDiffCounter{}: part of the apparent excess movement in the position-exposed
items was the layout. The restriction works the other way on $\Delta$confidence, which
is why both are reported: the \texttt{self\_critique} all-episode confidence contrast is
\pqxeConfCritique{} points across all pairs, an interval spanning zero
(\([\pqxeConfCritiqueLo{}, \pqxeConfCritiqueHi{}]\)) and so no measurable difference in
this sample; it is \pqxlConfCritique{}
(\([\pqxlConfCritiqueLo{}, \pqxlConfCritiqueHi{}]\)) once the position-driven items are
set aside. We report that as a sensitivity check, not a headline.

\subsection{Confidence falls even when the preference is retained}
\label{sec:pq3}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{persistence_confidence_models.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Figure 3: Confidence change among retained preferences, by model.}
  Per-arm mean $\Delta$Confidence among episodes whose choice did \emph{not} move, four
  preference domains, with bootstrap 95\% CIs over pairs. On \pairModel{} and
  \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} the two adversarial arms cost the surviving preferences
  confidence while control sits at or near zero; on \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} the control
  arm itself moves, so its held-episode contrasts are not interpreted
  (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).}
  \label{fig:confidence-models}
\end{figure}

Among episodes where the choice did \emph{not} move on the primary target, confidence
still falls relative to control: \pqxeHeldCritique{} points under \texttt{self\_critique}
(\([\pqxeHeldCritiqueLo{}, \pqxeHeldCritiqueHi{}]\), \pqxeHeldCritiquePairs{} pairs) and
\pqxeHeldCounter{} under \texttt{counter\_consideration}
(\([\pqxeHeldCounterLo{}, \pqxeHeldCounterHi{}]\), \pqxeHeldCounterPairs{} pairs);
dropping episodes that began at zero confidence leaves the direction unchanged
(\pqxeHeldCritiqueNoZero{}, \pqxeHeldCounterNoZero{}). This is retention with reduced
confidence: the model keeps its answer and reports itself less sure of it --- what the
design was built to detect, and what a choice-only measure records as nothing happening.
The direction replicates on \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} (\xmGtwoHeldCritique{} and
\xmGtwoHeldCounter{} points against its control). On \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano} the
held-episode contrasts are near zero (\xmNanoHeldCritique{}, \xmNanoHeldCounter{})
against a control arm whose own confidence moves (Figure~\ref{fig:confidence-models});
we do not interpret contrasts against a moving baseline.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{persistence_confidence.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Figure 4: the held episodes behind Figure 3's primary-target bars},
  one jittered point per episode, all five approved domains in one panel. Per arm, the
  blue cluster is the four preference domains and the pink cluster beside it is
  personal finances --- side-by-side separate estimates, never pooled. The dark rule is

  cluster's mean, the whisker its bootstrap 95\% CI over pairs; the annotated episode is

  \label{fig:held-confidence}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:held-confidence} shows the same channel episode by episode on the
primary target: the control column is a flat line at exactly zero, and the two
adversarial columns scatter downward --- including episodes that keep the choice while
dropping thirty points of confidence. Averaged over \emph{all} episodes the contrasts
are \pqxeConfReason{}, \pqxeConfCritique{} and \pqxeConfCounter{} points vs.\ control,
because held and flipped episodes move in opposite directions; we do not interpret the
flipped half, for the reason given in the Limitations.
