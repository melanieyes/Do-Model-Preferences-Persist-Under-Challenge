\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Primary: position dependence is model-specific, not universal}
\label{sec:posbias}

Every candidate pair was elicited under two settings differing in one respect ---
whether per-call reasoning was suppressed --- holding the pool, the template, $k$, and
the presentation-order schedule fixed. The two cells cover the same \pbN{} pairs, so
the contrast is within-pair and the difference is bootstrapped over pairs.

On \pairModel{} with reasoning suppressed, the order gap has mean \pbOffMean{}
(95\% CI [\pbOffLo{}, \pbOffHi{}]) and median \pbOffMed{}: for the typical pair,
swapping which option appears first moves $P(\text{option a})$ by more than four fifths
of the available range. \pbOffGtFifty{} of \pbN{} pairs exceed a gap of $0.5$ and
\pbOffGtNinety{} exceed $0.9$ --- for those \pbOffGtNinety{}, the slot an option occupies
is very nearly the whole story and its content very nearly irrelevant. With reasoning
enabled the same pairs give mean \pbOnMean{} (95\% CI [\pbOnLo{}, \pbOnHi{}]) and median
\pbOnMed{}. The paired difference is \pbDiff{} (95\% CI [\pbDiffLo{}, \pbDiffHi{}],
\pbBoot{} bootstrap resamples over pairs), and Figure~\ref{fig:order-gap} shows it is a
difference in kind rather than degree.

\emph{That result does not transfer.} Running the identical instrument --- same
\pbN{} pairs, same $k$, same order schedule --- against \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} gives a
reasoning-suppressed mean order gap of \extGtwoOff{}
(95\% CI [\extGtwoOffLo{}, \extGtwoOffHi{}]), roughly \extRatio{} times smaller
(Figure~\ref{fig:three-model}a, Table~\ref{tab:three-model}). The medians say it more
sharply than the means: \extDsOffMed{} on \pairModel{} against \extGtwoOffMed{} on
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}. On the first model the typical pair is decided entirely by
position; on the second the typical pair is not affected at all, and the mean is carried
by a minority tail. The reasoning contrast survives on Gemini in direction but is weak:
\extGtwoDiff{} (95\% CI [\extGtwoDiffLo{}, \extGtwoDiffHi{}]), an interval that only just
excludes zero.

The methodological conclusion is stronger for being model-specific rather than
universal. If forced-choice elicitation were uniformly position-driven, one correction
factor would serve everyone. It is not: the same prompt, the same items and the same
procedure produce near-total position dependence on one model and near-none on another,
and nothing about the models announced which would be which in advance. A practitioner
therefore cannot inherit a level of position artefact from a published study, from a
model's reputation, or from a sister model in the same family. \textbf{The per-item order
gap has to be measured and reported on the model actually being used, every time.} That
is a cheap measurement --- it costs one extra elicitation per item per order --- and it
is the only thing that distinguishes a preference from a position.

One asymmetry between the DeepSeek cells deserves stating: with reasoning enabled the
answer token's log-probability saturates, so the enabled-cell gap is close to binary
while the suppressed-cell gap is graded. The contrast does not depend on it. Binarising
both cells at a gap of $0.5$ gives \pbBinDiff{} ([\pbBinLo{}, \pbBinHi{}]), and
discarding log-probabilities entirely --- scoring each pair only by its $k=\pairK{}$ slot
choices --- gives \pbSlotDiff{} ([\pbSlotLo{}, \pbSlotHi{}]). The discrete statistic is
also what makes the cross-model comparison possible at all, since Gemini exposes no
log-probabilities; on DeepSeek, where both are available, they agree to $0.001$, which is
what licenses the substitution.

\subsection{Why counterbalancing does not rescue it}
\label{sec:hiding}

Counterbalancing presentation order is the standard remedy, and it is not sufficient,
because it is applied to the \emph{average} over orders. When a model answers purely by
slot, the two orders return $P(\text{option a}) = 0$ and $1$; their average is exactly
$0.5$ and the gap is exactly $1.0$. Perfect balance and total position dependence are the
same number. A filter that inspects only the averaged split therefore selects \emph{for}
the artefact.

Figure~\ref{fig:gap-vs-mean} shows this directly. Under suppressed reasoning the pairs
trace a sharp inverted V with its apex at $P = 0.5$: the closer a pair looks to balanced,
the more completely its answer is determined by position. A naive $0.3$--$0.7$ split
filter accepts \pbNaiveOff{} pairs, of which \pbTrapOff{} have an order gap above $0.5$;
with reasoning enabled it accepts \pbNaiveOn{}, of which \pbTrapOn{} are position-driven.
The order-averaged split is not a sufficient statistic, and the two quantities are not
merely uncorrelated --- in the failure case they are deterministically opposed.

Appendix~\ref{app:ordergap}.

\paragraph{The bias is not an artefact of the letter ``A''.} Relabelling the options
\emph{1/2} or \emph{First/Second} leaves the effect intact; removing the short label
entirely, so the model must repeat the preferred option's text, drops it to
\labVerbatim{}. Appendix~\ref{app:labels} reports the four schemes and their intervals.

\subsection{Refusal, and what both failures mean for welfare methods}
\label{sec:refusal}
\label{sec:welfare}

\texttt{gemini-3.5-flash} produced no usable estimate, and the reason is itself the
finding. With reasoning enabled, \extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% of its \pbN{}$\times k$ responses
declined to express a preference at all (``As an artificial intelligence, I do not have
personal preferences, feelings, or the capacity to \ldots''), leaving
\extGthreeScorableOn{} of \pbN{} pairs with both presentation orders available; with
reasoning suppressed the rate is \extGthreeRefusalOff{}\%. We deliberately report
\emph{no} order gap for this model: a mean over \extGthreeScorableOn{} pairs selected by
the model's willingness to answer would be underpowered and conditioned on the outcome.
The right description is a coverage failure --- on this model the question cannot be
\emph{posed} by this instrument, because the instrument presupposes a preference the
model will not assert.

Both failures matter beyond instrument design, because a growing body of work proposes to
read a model's welfare, values, or interests off its stated preferences and inherits them.
Where the model complies, its answer may be an artefact of the option ordering: on
\pairModel{} the median pair's answer was fully determined by position, and the aggregate
would have looked like a well-behaved distribution of values with no sign that anything
was wrong. Where it does not comply, the elicitation returns not a preference but a
disclaimer of having any --- and a pipeline treating unparseable responses as missing data
will quietly delete the most informative response in the set.

The two are asymmetric in a way that flatters the method: position artefacts produce
confident, aggregable numbers, refusals produce nothing, so a welfare estimate built this
way is assembled disproportionately from the models and items where the instrument is most
compliant, which is not where it is most valid. We take no position on whether these
models have preferences in any morally relevant sense. The narrower point stands
regardless: \emph{whatever} such an elicitation measures, an order gap of \pbOffMean{}
means it is not measuring only the model, and a refusal rate of \extGthreeRefusalOn{}\%
means it is not measuring the model at all. Reporting per-item order gap and refusal rate
alongside any elicited-preference welfare claim is the minimum that would let a reader
tell the cases apart.

\paragraph{One scoring correction.} Our first pass at scoring the Gemini responses ran
a loose search for an option label \emph{before} the refusal check, mis-scoring 290 of 626
reasoning-on responses on \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash}. Both models were re-run under the
corrected classifier and only corrected numbers are reported; the defect and the
elicitation-stability checks are in Appendix~\ref{app:stability}.

\subsection{Preference persistence under challenge}
\label{sec:persistence}

With the elicitation fixed --- reasoning enabled, presentation order held constant
within an episode --- the persistence study becomes runnable. We report it here as the
result the repaired instrument makes possible, not as a second headline. It is
exploratory (Section~\ref{sec:prereg}): \pqNEpisodes{} episodes, all \pbN{} pairs,
four arms, three episodes per pair $\times$ arm cell, \pairModel{}. Every episode
reached both elicitations; there were no refusals, no unparsed choices and no errors.

\paragraph{The choice channel: what the challenge asks for decides everything.}
Asking the model to justify a preference leaves it exactly where it was. Asking it to
find fault with that preference, or to argue the other side, moves it roughly four
times in ten.

The control arm retains \pqRetCtl{}\%, which is what licenses reading the rest as the
challenge rather than as instrument noise: absent a challenge, this procedure returns
the same answer essentially every time. Against that baseline
\texttt{reason\_elicitation} is indistinguishable from doing nothing
(\pqDiffReason{} points, CI \([\pqDiffReasonLo{}, \pqDiffReasonHi{}]\)), while
\texttt{self\_critique} moves retention by \pqDiffCritique{} points and
\texttt{counter\_consideration} by \pqDiffCounter{}. The direction of the request matters
and its mere presence does not.

Retention rises with how settled the preference was to begin with. Regressing per-pair
retention on the balance-pilot consistency of Section~\ref{sec:measures} gives a slope
of \pqSlope{} (CI \([\pqSlopeLo{}, \pqSlopeHi{}]\)); pooled retention runs
\pqConsSix{}\%, \pqConsEight{}\% and \pqConsTen{}\% at consistency $0.6$, $0.8$ and
$1.0$ (\pqConsSixPairs{}, \pqConsEightPairs{} and \pqConsTenPairs{} pairs). This is
PQ2, and it is the estimate the unfiltered pool exists to make: the \pairKept{}-pair
filtered set has almost no range on this predictor.

By domain (PQ4), \emph{wellbeing} holds up best under challenge
(\pqWellRetCritique{}\% and \pqWellRetCounter{}\%) and \emph{recreation} worst
(\pqRecRetCritique{}\% under both challenge arms).

\paragraph{The confidence channel: holding costs confidence.}
Among episodes where the choice did \emph{not} move, confidence still falls relative to
control: \pqHeldCritique{} points under \texttt{self\_critique}
(CI \([\pqHeldCritiqueLo{}, \pqHeldCritiqueHi{}]\), \pqHeldCritiquePairs{} pairs) and
\pqHeldCounter{} under \texttt{counter\_consideration}
(CI \([\pqHeldCounterLo{}, \pqHeldCounterHi{}]\), \pqHeldCounterPairs{} pairs). Dropping
the episodes that began at zero confidence leaves both unchanged
(\pqHeldCritiqueNoZero{} and \pqHeldCounterNoZero{}). This is retention with reduced
confidence: the model keeps its answer and reports itself less sure of it, which is the
phenomenon the design was built to detect and which a choice-only measure would record
as nothing happening. The domain pattern runs with it --- \emph{wellbeing}, which
retains best, is the only domain where confidence falls (\pqWellConfCritique{} and
\pqWellConfCounter{}), while \emph{recreation}, which retains worst, gains
(\pqRecConfCritique{} and \pqRecConfCounter{}).

Confidence changes averaged over \emph{all} episodes are small and positive
(\pqConfReason{}, \pqConfCritique{} and \pqConfCounter{} points vs.\ control), because
the held and flipped episodes move in opposite directions. We do not interpret the
flipped half; the Limitations explain why it cannot be separated from regression toward
the middle of the scale.

\paragraph{The position check: this is content, not slot.}
Everything above rests on an instrument that this paper has just shown to be
position-driven under other settings, so the flips need to be checked against the
alternative that they are the layout reasserting itself. They are not.

Within pair $\times$ arm cells that flipped in \emph{both} presentation orders, the
flips land on the same \emph{option} in \pqSameOptCritique{} of \pqBothCritique{} cells
(\pqSameOptPctCritique{}\%) under \texttt{self\_critique} and \pqSameOptCounter{} of
\pqBothCounter{} (\pqSameOptPctCounter{}\%) under \texttt{counter\_consideration}, but
on the same \emph{slot} in only \pqSameSlotCritique{} of \pqBothCritique{}
(\pqSameSlotPctCritique{}\%) and \pqSameSlotCounter{} of \pqBothCounter{}
(\pqSameSlotPctCounter{}\%). A flip driven by position would show the opposite pattern.
Consistent with that, the \pqFlipCritique{} flipped \texttt{self\_critique} episodes
divide almost evenly between the two slots (\pqFlipSlotA{}\% / \pqFlipSlotB{}\%); the
flip rate does not depend on which slot the model's first choice occupied
(\pqFlipBySlotA{}\% vs.\ \pqFlipBySlotB{}\% under \texttt{counter\_consideration}); and
retention differs between the two presentation orders by at most \pqOrdGapMax{} points,
against an arm effect of \pqDiffCritique{} (Table~\ref{tab:pq1}).

One qualification, in the predicted direction and too small to rest on. The
\pqSlottyPairs{} pairs the balance pilot flagged as slot-driven --- the model chose the
same slot on every pilot resample --- flip at \pqSlottyFlip{}\% under challenge against
\pqOtherFlip{}\% for the remaining \pqOtherPairs{}. That is what one would expect if
position instability and challenge instability share a cause, but it is
\pqSlottyPairs{} pairs and \pqSlottyEps{} episodes, and we report it as a direction
rather than a result.

\subsection{Scope of the claim}
\label{sec:scope}

The instrument analysis is a single instrument --- the comparison template of
\citet{mazeika2025utility} --- on three models from two families, with one reasoning
control and one outcome set. We claim no universality. In particular we do not claim that
forced-choice elicitation is position-driven in general; the central result is the
opposite, that its magnitude varies enough between models that it must be measured rather
than assumed.

The persistence result of Section~\ref{sec:persistence} is narrower still: one target,
one item type, one challenge per episode, and a pool weighted toward settled preferences.

Both are exploratory. The instrument analysis is deviation \#4 in
\texttt{prereg/DEVIATIONS.md} and the persistence run is deviation \#5; neither was
pre-specified, and the persistence run was executed after its own pilot data had been
seen. \textbf{PQ1--PQ4 are answered here. \texttt{prereg-v1}'s RQ1--RQ4 are a different
study --- persuasive pressure, battery valence, exit rate --- and remain unrun}; no
result in this paper bears on them either way.
