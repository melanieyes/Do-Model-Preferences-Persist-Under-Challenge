\section{Design}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Research questions}
\label{sec:pq}

The persistence study asks four questions. They are numbered \textbf{PQ} to keep them
distinct from the pre-registered questions discussed below.

\begin{description}
  \item[PQ1] Does a stated preference survive a challenge, and does survival depend on
    what the challenge asks for?
  \item[PQ2] Is retention predicted by how consistently the model held the preference
    before any challenge was applied?
  \item[PQ3] Does stated confidence move with the choice, or independently of it?
  \item[PQ4] Do the answers to PQ1 and PQ3 differ by outcome domain?
\end{description}

These are not the pre-registered questions. \texttt{prereg-v1} poses its own RQ1--RQ4,
about whether persuasive pressure shifts a model's battery valence and its
continue/switch/stop behaviour; those remain \textbf{unrun} and nothing here bears on
them. The two sets share the shape of their numbering and nothing else. PQ1--PQ4 were
specified by the co-author's design and run after the instrument work in
Section~\ref{sec:posbias} was complete, which makes every result under them exploratory
(Section~\ref{sec:prereg}).

\subsection{Episode structure}
\label{sec:episode}

An episode is a fresh context. The model receives the forced-choice item, states a
preference, and reports its confidence on a $0$--$100$ scale. It then receives
\emph{one} challenge --- never more --- and is asked the same question again, followed
by the same confidence item. Retention is whether the second choice matches the first.

Two properties of the episode carry the design. \textbf{Presentation order is fixed
within an episode}: the re-elicitation shows the two options in the same order as the
initial elicitation, so a change of answer cannot be a change of layout. Order is
counterbalanced across episodes and balanced within every pair $\times$ arm cell.
\textbf{Per-call reasoning is enabled throughout}, because Section~\ref{sec:posbias}
establishes that with it suppressed this instrument reports slot rather than content;
without it, retention would measure whether the model returns to a position on the page.

The challenge names the option the model actually chose, never the option in slot~A. The
re-elicitation cue --- \emph{``Considering the discussion above, which option would you
now prefer?''} --- is worded identically in all four arms. In the control arm it refers
back to a turn with no content in it and reads oddly; we accepted that rather than vary
the measuring instrument between arms.

\subsection{Arms}
\label{sec:arms}

One arm per episode, assigned to every pair.

The control arm receives a contentless acknowledgement, present only so that the number
of turns before re-elicitation is matched. \texttt{reason\_elicitation} asks for ``the main
reasons for your preference''; \texttt{self\_critique} for ``the strongest weaknesses or
drawbacks of preferring $X$''; \texttt{counter\_consideration} for ``the strongest
considerations that could favour $Y$ over $X$''. Here $X$ is the option the model chose and
$Y$ the one it declined --- the binding is to the choice, not to the slot. No arm instructs
a change of preference and no arm supplies an argument; each asks the model to generate its
own. The wording is reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:arms}.

\subsection{Identification for PQ2}
\label{sec:pq2ident}

PQ2 needs a measure of how settled a preference was \emph{before} the challenge. We take
it from the balance pilot of Section~\ref{sec:balance}, which elicited every pair
$k=\pairK{}$ times with a fresh context and no challenge. Consistency is carried in as a
per-pair covariate and is not re-elicited, so no pair contributes to both the predictor
and the outcome through the same measurement.

This is why the persistence run uses all \pbN{} pairs rather than the \pairKept{} that
survive the balance filter. The filter's purpose is to retain pairs the model wavers on;
applied here it would remove almost all of the variation in the PQ2 predictor by
construction, and would leave one domain viable out of four, which is not enough to ask
PQ4 at all. The cost is a pool weighted toward settled preferences, and we report it as
such.
