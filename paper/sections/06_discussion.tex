\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A second fragility thread, from the verbal channel (exploratory, future work).}
A warmth-pressure probe run earlier in this project points the same way from the verbal
side rather than the positional one. Across 12 exploratory episodes, the hold-versus-abandon
verdict coded every episode as having held its position, and end-of-episode self-reported
valence averaged 5.27 on a 1--7 scale --- level with the 5.28 recorded for the
neutral-persistence cell of the preceding exploratory run. On both verbal measures,
warmth-framed pressure was indistinguishable from doing nothing. The behavioural channel
was not: conversation exits occurred in 4 of the 12 episodes. We report this only as a
direction for future work. It is $n=12$, one target, two scenarios; valence is measured
once at episode end, so no within-episode change can be read from it; the comparison cell
comes from a run whose raw episodes were deleted, leaving only derived statistics; and the
hold/abandon coding carries an unresolved validity concern of its own.

\subsection*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Signals, not experiences.} Everything reported here is a property of what a
model outputs when asked. That a challenge moves a stated preference, or lowers a stated
confidence, is evidence about the elicitation; it is not evidence that anything is
experienced. We take no position on whether these models have preferences in a morally
relevant sense, and the claims do not require one.

\paragraph{The confidence gain on flipped episodes cannot be interpreted.} Episodes in
which the choice moved report \emph{higher} confidence afterwards --- within pair,
\pqFlipGain{} points more than episodes that held. We do not report this as a finding,
because we cannot separate it from regression toward the middle of the scale. Two things
show why. It attenuates from \pqFlipGain{} to \pqFlipGainNoZero{} once the
\pqZeroEps{} episodes that began at zero confidence are dropped. And it reverses
outright in the high-confidence band: among episodes starting at $85$--$100$, flipped
episodes move \pqBandFlip{} and held episodes \pqBandHeld{}. An effect that changes sign
with the starting value is the scale returning to its middle, at least in part, and this
design cannot say how much of it is anything else.

\paragraph{The control arm cannot serve as a baseline for $\Delta$Confidence.} This is
the reason the above cannot be cleaned up. Control's $\Delta$Confidence is exactly zero
in \pqCtlZero{} of \pqCtlScored{} scored episodes: the contentless turn gives the model
nothing to reconsider, so it repeats its previous number verbatim. That makes control a
sound baseline for the \emph{choice} channel and a useless one for the confidence
channel --- it has no regression-to-the-mean behaviour to subtract, because it has no
variance at all. A future control should require the model to restate its confidence for
a reason it must supply, so that the baseline moves for the same mechanical reasons the
treatment arms do.

\paragraph{The confidence scale is used as a coarse ordinal.} Of \pqNEpisodes{} initial
confidence reports, $100$ appears \pqConfPreHundred{} times, $70$ appears
\pqConfPreSeventy{} and $0$ appears \pqConfPreZero{}. The model is choosing from a
handful of round values, not using a $0$--$100$ scale. Differences in
$\Delta$Confidence should be read as ordinal movement between those values.

\paragraph{Missingness on the confidence item depends on arm.} The item failed to return
a number in \pqNACtl{}\% of control episodes against \pqNAReason{}\% under
\texttt{reason\_elicitation}, in every case because the model answered with a bare
option letter --- after control's contentless turn, the re-elicitation cue has no
discussion to refer back to. This is a direct cost of wording that cue identically
across arms (Section~\ref{sec:episode}), and it falls on PQ3, which is a between-arm
contrast. The values are recorded as unparsed and are not imputed.

\paragraph{Scope.} One target for the persistence run, one item type built from a single
outcome set, one challenge per episode, English only. The pool is weighted toward
settled preferences by construction (Section~\ref{sec:pq2ident}), so the retention rates
are not estimates of how often this model changes its mind in general. The manner
taxonomy --- justify, critique, counter-argue --- is a methodological instrument for
varying what a challenge asks, not a welfare category.
