\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A growing body of work reads something about a language model off its stated preferences:
utility models fitted to pairwise choices \citep{mazeika2025utility}, and welfare research
working from self-reported affect \citep{anthropic2026emotions, anthropic2025card} or
behavioural analogues such as ending an interaction
\citep{ensign2025bail, lesswrong2026bail}. The forced choice is the workhorse --- two
outcomes, labelled A and B, a request for one letter. It is cheap, it parses, and it
aggregates. It also has no correct answer, which is the point of asking, and which means
that when the procedure goes wrong there is no accuracy to drop and nothing to notice.

We set out to build such items, adapting the outcome set of \citet{mazeika2025utility}
into \pbN{} within-category pairs, in order to study whether a stated preference survives
being challenged. The instrument failed before the study began. Diagnosing that failure is
the bulk of this paper; repairing it is what let the original study run.

Eliciting each pair \pairK{} times with a fresh context and the presentation order
counterbalanced, we find that on \pairModel{} with per-call reasoning suppressed the
median pair's answer is determined \emph{entirely} by which slot an option occupies.
Enabling reasoning removes most of it. That alone might be filed alongside known order
effects in multiple-choice answering and LLM-as-judge evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:related}). Three things make it worth reporting on its own.

\textbf{Counterbalancing --- the standard remedy --- conceals this failure rather than
fixing it.} A pair answered purely by slot returns $P(\text{option a}) = 0$ in one order
and $1$ in the other, averaging to exactly $0.5$: the value that reads as a perfectly
balanced preference. Selecting items on the order-averaged split therefore selects
\emph{for} the artefact, and we show it doing so on real data
(Section~\ref{sec:hiding}). \textbf{The magnitude is model-specific and unpredictable in
advance.} The identical instrument on identical items gives a mean order gap of
\pbOffMean{} on \pairModel{} and \extGtwoOff{} on \texttt{gemini-2.5-flash} --- medians
\extDsOffMed{} and \extGtwoOffMed{}. Nothing about the two models announced which would be
which, so the gap has to be measured on the model in use rather than inherited from a
published figure or a sister model. \textbf{A second failure mode is invisible to the same
pipelines.} \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash} declines to assign itself preferences in
\extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% of trials, leaving \extGthreeScorableOn{} of \pbN{} pairs
scoreable; an analysis dropping unparseable responses as missing data would record it as
clean.

These two failure modes are asymmetric in a way that flatters the method: position
artefacts yield confident, aggregable numbers, refusals yield nothing. Any welfare or
alignment claim built on elicited preferences is therefore assembled disproportionately
from the models and items where the instrument is most compliant, which is not where it is
most valid. We take no position on whether these models have preferences in any morally
relevant sense; the narrower claim does not need one. Whatever such elicitation measures,
an order gap of \pbOffMean{} means it is not measuring only the model, and a refusal rate
of \extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% means it is not measuring the model at all
(Section~\ref{sec:welfare}).

With the elicitation repaired --- reasoning enabled, presentation order fixed within an
episode --- the persistence study the items were built for becomes runnable, and we report
it as PQ1--PQ4 (Section~\ref{sec:persistence}). Across \pqNEpisodes{} episodes, what a
challenge \emph{asks for} decides whether a stated preference survives it, the preferences
that survive are held with measurably less confidence, and the flips track the option
rather than the slot. All elicitation records, the sampling seed, the exclusion rules and
the upstream file-level commit are released, so the selection is auditable end to end.

\paragraph{Status.} Neither result was pre-specified: the position-bias finding came out
of instrument work rather than a stated hypothesis, and the persistence run was executed
afterwards with its own pilot data already inspected. Both are exploratory, and the value
of either is as a hypothesis for a pre-specified replication.
