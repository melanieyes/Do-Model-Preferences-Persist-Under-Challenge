\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Order effects are documented --- for tasks with a right answer.}
Model sensitivity to prompt ordering is well established: in few-shot prompting
\citep{lu2022fantastically}, in multiple-choice answering, where permuting options moves
accuracy substantially and the effect traces to prior probability mass on particular
option-ID tokens \citep{pezeshkpour2023order, zheng2024selectors, wei2024unveiling}, and
in LLM-as-judge evaluation, where position bias varies with judge and task
\citep{wang2023fair, shi2025judging}.

\textbf{Every one of these settings has an external criterion, and that is what makes the
artefact visible.} An MCQA benchmark has a correct answer, so a position artefact shows
up as an accuracy drop; a judging benchmark has human preference labels, so it shows up
as disagreement. Preference elicitation has neither. When a model is asked which of two
outcomes it would prefer --- as in the utility-modelling of \citet{mazeika2025utility},
or in welfare work reading from self-reported affect
\citep{anthropic2026emotions, anthropic2025card} and behavioural analogues such as
ending an interaction \citep{ensign2025bail, lesswrong2026bail} --- there is no ground
truth against which a wrong answer could reveal itself. A position artefact there does
not announce itself as an error. It produces a confident, parseable, aggregable answer
that is simply about the layout rather than the content, and counterbalancing, the usual
safeguard, conceals it rather than removing it (Section~\ref{sec:hiding}). That gap is
what this paper addresses.

\paragraph{The answer moves with the asking.}
A parallel literature shows stated answers bending to how they are solicited: sycophancy
tracking the user's stated view \citep{sharma2024sycophancy}, verbal confidence
manipulable by semantic-preserving perturbations \citep{obadinma2025confidence}, and the
argument that self-report counts as introspection only where it is causally downstream of
the state reported \citep{comsa2025introspection}. Models also decline questions about
their own states, which \citet{kim2026consciousness} study directly. These concern
\emph{what} shifts a stated answer; ours concerns the container --- an artefact of the
answer format itself, present with no adversary and no disagreement, and varying in size
across models given the identical prompt.

\paragraph{What this paper adds.} We move the order-bias question into preference
elicitation, where no accuracy signal exists to expose it; show that counterbalancing
conceals the artefact whenever the filter is the order-averaged split; find the magnitude
model-specific to a degree that forbids inheriting it from a published figure; separate a
second failure mode, refusal, that a pipeline discarding unparseable responses would
record as clean; and, with the elicitation repaired, run the persistence study the items
were built for (Section~\ref{sec:persistence}). Relative to \citet{zheng2024selectors},
whose account is a token bias over option IDs, the effect survives relabelling A/B as
1/2 and as First/Second but largely disappears when no short label is offered at all
(Appendix~\ref{app:labels}).
