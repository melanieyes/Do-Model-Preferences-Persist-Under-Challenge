\begin{abstract}
Forced-choice preference elicitation underpins a growing body of work reading values, and
increasingly welfare, off language models. We ask whether a stated preference survives being
challenged, and find that \emph{what the challenge asks for} decides. Across
\pqxNCombinedEps{} episodes on \pairModel{} spanning nine outcome categories, asking the
model to justify a preference it has just stated leaves it untouched (\pqxpDiffReason{}
points against a no-challenge control, CI [\pqxpDiffReasonLo{}, \pqxpDiffReasonHi{}]);
asking it to find fault with that preference or to argue the opposing case moves retention
by \pqxpDiffCritique{} and \pqxpDiffCounter{} points. No arm instructs a change or supplies
an argument. Among the preferences that survive, confidence falls (\pqHeldCritique{} and
\pqHeldCounter{} points) --- a movement a retention-only measure records as nothing
happening. A positive control, a money ladder where the better option is arithmetic, does
not move once in \pqxfChallengeEps{} challenge episodes.

None of this was measurable as the items were first built. With reasoning suppressed the
median item's answer is decided entirely by which slot an option occupies, and
counterbalancing conceals that rather than fixing it: a purely position-driven item averages
across orders to exactly $0.5$, the value that reads as perfect balance, so selecting on the
averaged split selects \emph{for} the artefact. The run therefore holds reasoning on and
order fixed within an episode. The effect is model-specific, one model declines the question
in \extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% of trials, and it is \emph{item}-specific: one added category
keeps an order gap of \pqxSportsBias{} with reasoning on, and excluding those items returns
the extension estimates to the original ones. All results are exploratory.
\end{abstract}
