\begin{abstract}
Forced-choice preference elicitation underpins a growing body of work that reads values,
and increasingly welfare, off language models. We run one such instrument --- \pbN{} item
pairs drawn verbatim from the outcome set of \citet{mazeika2025utility} --- unchanged
across three model instances, and find that how much it measures the model rather than
the layout of the question is \emph{model-specific and not predictable in advance}. With
per-call reasoning suppressed, the mean order gap (how far $P(\text{option a})$ moves when
the two options swap positions) is \pbOffMean{} on \pairModel{} but \extGtwoOff{} on
\texttt{gemini-2.5-flash}; the medians are \extDsOffMed{} and \extGtwoOffMed{}, so on the
first model the typical item is decided entirely by position and on the second it is not
affected at all. Enabling reasoning shrinks the effect on \pairModel{} by \pbDiff{}
(95\% CI [\pbDiffLo{}, \pbDiffHi{}]). Counterbalancing does not repair this, it conceals
it: a purely position-driven pair averages to exactly $0.5$ across orders, so the items
that look most balanced are the ones most determined by position, and a naive split
filter accepts \pbTrapOff{} such pairs out of \pbNaiveOff{}. Relabelling the options
\emph{1/2} or \emph{First/Second} leaves the effect intact; requiring the model to repeat
the preferred option's text instead of naming a label drops it to \labVerbatim{}. A third
model, \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash}, declines to assign itself preferences in
\extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% of trials, so the question cannot be posed on it at all. Because
the magnitude varies this much with nothing to anticipate it from, the per-item order gap
must be measured on the model in use rather than assumed --- and any welfare or alignment
method built on elicited stated preferences inherits both failure modes, one of which
produces confident numbers and the other of which produces nothing.

With the elicitation repaired --- reasoning enabled, presentation order held fixed within
an episode --- the persistence study the items were built for becomes runnable, and we
report it: across \pqNEpisodes{} episodes, asking the model to justify a stated preference
leaves it untouched (\pqDiffReason{} points against control), while asking it to critique
that preference or argue the opposing case moves retention by \pqDiffCritique{} and
\pqDiffCounter{} points. Among the choices that do survive, confidence falls
(\pqHeldCritique{} and \pqHeldCounter{} points). Flips track the option rather than the
slot, so the effect is content and not the position artefact returning. These questions are
labelled PQ1--PQ4 throughout; the pre-registered RQ1--RQ4 are a different study and remain
unrun.
\end{abstract}
