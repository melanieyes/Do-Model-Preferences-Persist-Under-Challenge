\begin{abstract}
Forced-choice preference elicitation underpins a growing body of work reading values, and
increasingly welfare, off language models. We ask whether a stated preference survives being
challenged. \emph{What the challenge asks for} decides. Across \pqxNCombinedEps{} episodes
on \pairModel{} spanning nine outcome categories, asking the model to justify a preference
it has just stated leaves it untouched (\pqxpDiffReason{} points against a no-challenge
control, CI [\pqxpDiffReasonLo{}, \pqxpDiffReasonHi{}]). Asking it to find fault with that
preference or to argue the opposing case moves retention by \pqxpDiffCritique{} and
\pqxpDiffCounter{} points. No arm instructs a change or supplies an argument. Among the
preferences that survive, confidence falls (\pqHeldCritique{} and \pqHeldCounter{} points).
A retention-only measure records that as nothing happening. A positive control, a money
ladder where the better option is arithmetic, does not move once in \pqxfChallengeEps{}
challenge episodes. The arm pattern holds on \pqmNTargets{} model families. The rank of the
two adversarial arms does not.

None of this was measurable as the items were first built. With reasoning suppressed the
median item's answer is decided entirely by which slot an option occupies. Counterbalancing
conceals that rather than fixing it. A purely position-driven item averages across orders to
exactly $0.5$, the value that reads as perfect balance, so selecting on the averaged split
selects \emph{for} the artefact. The run therefore holds reasoning on and order fixed within
an episode. The effect is model-specific, and one model declines the question in
\extGthreeRefusalOn{}\% of trials. It is \emph{item}-specific too. One added category keeps
an order gap of \pqxSportsBias{} with reasoning on, and excluding those items returns the
extension estimates to the original ones. All results are exploratory.
\end{abstract}
